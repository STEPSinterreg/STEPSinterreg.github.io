# Hearing Loss Lesson Plan, Final Draft v1

## Lesson goal
Pupils should experience hearing loss in a short, active way. The lesson should be easy to follow, physically engaging, and built around listening, comparing, and noticing what changes.

## Recommended length
20-30 minutes for the core lesson.

## Lesson summary
Use three parts only:
1. Quick compare mode.
2. Guided hearing-loss experience.
3. Live microphone mode.

## Copy-paste lesson text

### Introduction
In this lesson, we will explore how hearing loss can sound. You will listen, compare, and try a live microphone mode so you can hear the effect for yourself.

### Part 1: Start by standing up
Begin away from the screen.
Ask the pupils to stand and complete one very short listening task.
Use a sound cue, a hand signal, or a simple movement response.
Keep it playful and quick.

Purpose: get attention, create movement, and set the lesson up as active rather than passive.

### Part 2: Quick compare mode
Use the "Hear and Compare" section first.
Let pupils hear one sound through different hearing-loss profiles.
Keep it to one or two comparisons only.
Ask one short question, for example: "What changed?" or "Which one sounds clearest?"

Purpose: give a fast, intuitive first understanding of how hearing loss can sound.

### Part 3: Live microphone mode
Use the live microphone section as the second main activity.
Pupils speak, clap, or read one short word into the microphone.
The app processes the sound immediately through a hearing-loss profile.
Tell pupils clearly that headphones are strongly recommended, preferably sound-isolating headphones.
Keep the volume low.

Purpose: make the experience personal and immediate, so pupils hear the effect on their own voice and sounds.

### Part 4: Short guided experience
Use only the essential guided part of the simulator.
Keep the number of steps small.
Focus on one listen-and-test sequence.
Avoid long reading tasks.
Explain key words orally instead of expecting pupils to read a lot.

Purpose: keep the lesson manageable for younger pupils.

### Part 5: Short reflection
End with one simple question.
What was easiest to hear?
What changed the most?
What was hardest to understand?

Purpose: close the lesson with one short discussion instead of a long wrap-up.

## What to keep on the webpage
Keep the parts that already work visually and pedagogically:
- The compare cards with the frequency response curves.
- The audiogram graph in the guided experience.
- The simple hearing-loss icons for the main entry points.
- The audio clips for speech, street noise, and birds.

## What to remove or reduce
- Long text blocks.
- Any explanation that makes the lesson feel like a lecture.
- Open-ended tasks where pupils can stop at "I do not know".
- English videos in the core lesson flow.

## Graphics and videos

### Graphics to keep
Use the current compare graphics already in the app.
- The hearing-loss cards already show a simple frequency response curve for each profile.
- That is enough for the compare mode.
- In the guided section, keep the audiogram chart as the main visual.

### How to set them up in the lesson platform
If you are entering the lesson plan manually, present the visuals like this:
- Show one compare card at a time, or use a short 2x2 comparison table.
- Use the curve graphic only as a visual explanation of the sound difference.
- Use the audiogram graph later, after pupils have heard the change.
- Keep the live microphone mode mostly text-light and use one warning box for headphones.

### Videos
I do not recommend keeping the current English videos in the core pupil lesson.
My recommendation is:
- Remove them from the main lesson flow.
- If you want video, use Danish video instead.
- If no good Danish video exists, keep video as an optional teacher resource only.

Reason: the lesson is already at risk of becoming too text-heavy. English videos will probably make the content harder for children to access.

## Teacher note
The hearing-loss experience itself is the strongest part of the project. The lesson should build around that experience, not around long explanations or video content.

## Final recommendation
If you want the lesson to work well for younger pupils, keep the content short, use the new live microphone mode, and let the visuals do more of the teaching than the text.
