# Draft: Improved Lesson Plan for the Hearing Loss Simulator

## Goal
Give pupils a short, active introduction to hearing loss, and make them move, listen, compare, and talk about what they notice.

## Recommended length
20-30 minutes for the core lesson.

## Core idea
Keep the lesson focused on three simple modes:
1. Quick comparison of hearing loss types.
2. A short guided hearing-loss experience.
3. A live microphone mode where pupils hear their own voice or sounds through a hearing-loss profile.

## Suggested lesson flow

### 1. Start standing, not sitting
Begin with a 1-2 minute physical listening task.
- Ask pupils to stand up.
- Have them listen for a sound cue from the teacher.
- Ask them to move one step forward, sideways, or raise a hand when they hear a change.
- Keep the task simple and playful.

Purpose: get attention, reset energy, and make the topic feel active before anyone looks at the screen.

### 2. Quick compare mode
Use the "Hear and Compare" mode first.
- Let pupils hear one sound through different hearing-loss profiles.
- Keep the comparison short.
- Ask one question only, for example: "What changed?" or "Which profile sounds clearest?"

Purpose: give a fast, intuitive example of hearing-loss differences.

### 3. Live microphone mode
Use a microphone mode as the new third section.
- Pupils can speak, clap, or read a short word into the microphone.
- The app plays the sound back through a hearing-loss profile in real time.
- Tell pupils clearly that headphones are strongly recommended, preferably sound-isolating headphones.
- Remind them to keep the volume low.

Purpose: make the experience personal and immediate, and let pupils hear their own voice change.

### 4. Short guided experience
Use only the essential part of the guided experience.
- Focus on one short listen-and-test sequence.
- Do not require pupils to read long explanations.
- Let the teacher explain the key terms out loud.
- Keep the number of levels small in the classroom version.

Purpose: keep the lesson manageable for younger pupils.

### 5. End with one short reflection
Ask pupils to answer one simple question.
- What was easiest to hear?
- What changed the most?
- Which sound was hardest to understand?

## Content changes recommended for the webpage
- Keep the hearing-loss simulator, but shorten the visible lesson path.
- Add the live microphone mode as a third entry point.
- Replace long text blocks with short prompts.
- Add click-for-explanation on important words.
- Use more audio and fewer paragraphs.
- Keep the controls clearly labeled as play, pause, and stop.
- Avoid open-ended tasks that let pupils stop at "I do not know" without help.

## Video recommendation
Do not keep English videos in the core lesson unless they are clearly short, simple, and subtitled in Danish.

My recommendation:
- Remove the current English videos from the main lesson flow.
- If video is still useful, replace it with Danish material or short Danish captions.
- If no good Danish video exists, move video to an optional teacher resource instead of the pupil-facing flow.

Reason:
The feedback suggests the lesson may already be too text-heavy and too advanced. English videos are likely to add another language barrier instead of helping the main learning goal.

## Teacher note
The strongest part of the project is still the hearing-loss experience itself. The lesson should build around that, not around long explanations.
